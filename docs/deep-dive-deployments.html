Deployments

The Deployment Status Lifecycle

Deployments is the richest state machine in the app. A seasonal display doesn't just get "set up" — it moves through five distinct stages, and every item, zone, and power connection inside it advances in lockstep. This is the sub that makes "what's actually deployed right now, and what's still boxed?" a question with a real-time answer instead of a guess.

1

Plan

A deployment is created for a season and year, with three fixed zones — front yard, back yard, side yard. This is the planning surface: deciding what goes where before anything physically moves.

2

Stage

Storage totes — and, for large or awkward props that don't live in a tote at all, individual items — are pulled and marked staged. This is the only point where Deployments reaches into Storage, and it does so by reading eligible items directly rather than duplicating what Storage already knows.

3

Deploy

Work sessions track who's actively setting up and when. Items get placed into zones and wired to power connections one at a time; completing the deployment bulk-advances every staged item to deployed in one step — and is blocked outright if a session is still open, so a deployment can't be marked done while someone's mid-setup.

4

Teardown

A dedicated teardown phase walks items back out of the display — the reverse of staging, not a delete. Items return to storage status rather than disappearing from the system.

5

Archive

Once torn down, a deployment becomes historical — queryable on its own, with the season's full zone/session/connection layout preserved for next year's planning instead of thrown away.

Notable decision

Item status isn't a field Deployments merely reads — it's a field Deployments actively drives. As a deployment moves through each stage, the items inside it are pushed through their own status transitions in the same operation. That coupling is deliberate: it's what lets Items, Storage, and Workbench all show a consistent, real-time picture of "where is this thing right now" without a separate reconciliation process running behind the scenes.
